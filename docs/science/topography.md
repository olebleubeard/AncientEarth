# Optional downloaded elevation surfaces

The default surface remains the smooth WGS84 ellipsoid, `h=0`. The manual elevation downloader can replace finite covered regions with a canonical triangle mesh from **Copernicus DEM GLO-30 Public, AWS 2021 release**. Turning Elevations off restores the smooth model and keeps the downloaded files. No city catalog or photographic layer is involved. Height-free land/water categories remain an independent geographic classification; they never overwrite downloaded elevations.

## Source, rights and meaning

The [official AWS registry](https://registry.opendata.aws/copernicus-dem/) identifies this release and its limited global coverage. Its [processing description](https://copernicus-dem-30m.s3.amazonaws.com/readme.html) specifies the TIFF structure, latitude-dependent longitude spacing, removed duplicate edge posts and averaged overviews. The first, full-resolution image is used; averaged overviews are rejected. Missing files can indicate ocean coverage or other unavailable source areas. An HTTP 404 is **not** evidence that a point is ocean or has zero physical height.

Copernicus is a **digital surface model**, including effects of vegetation, infrastructure and buildings, rather than a bare-earth survey. The [publisher's collection specification](https://dataspace.copernicus.eu/explore-data/data-collections/copernicus-contributing-missions/collections-description/COP-DEM) identifies WGS84-G1150 horizontal coordinates and EGM2008 heights. The primary TanDEM-X observations date from 2011–2015, with other source epochs in gap fills. Published accuracy statistics do not bound every retained post, an interpolated triangle, or present-day ground. The application does not transform WGS84 realizations or update source terrain to today's date.

Before acquiring an elevation file, the transport verifies that its actual package EULA matches the reviewed [Copernicus license](../../licenses/Copernicus-WorldDEM-30-eula_F.pdf). Source URLs, byte counts, SHA-256 hashes, package metadata, attribution and the license are retained in the cache manifest. The known EULA hash is `32049914c37f14e7d53b48d13d74a49e77c030236e2acc7a0df426f9344feba2`. A changed or unknown license fails closed. [NOTICE](../../NOTICE.md) records modification and redistribution obligations; public access alone is not permission.

## Radius and conservative query bounds

The editable distance is a **surface-distance radius**, not a diameter, altitude or viewing range. The default and maximum are exactly `100 international miles = 160,934.4 m = 160.9344 km`; inputs must be positive. The conversion, geographic request constructor and source planner enforce this limit, including saved selections. Whole source tiles and seam-support neighbors can still extend beyond that requested radius. The international foot is exactly `0.3048 m`, the inch is `0.0254 m`, and a mile is `5280 ft`; see [NIST's definitions and conversions](https://www.nist.gov/pml/us-surveyfoot/revised-unit-conversion-factors). Switching display units preserves the physical radius. The exact decimal metric maximum is accepted and canonicalized to the same stored metre value despite floating-point conversion rounding; larger displayed or stored radii are rejected.

Right-pane Imperial eye-height and datum readouts round the absolute height to the nearest whole inch, with halfway values rounded away from zero, then divide into whole feet and a remainder from zero through eleven inches. A leading minus sign applies to the complete feet/inches value, including negative heights below one foot; a rounded-zero primary value has no minus sign. The parenthetical miles retain the original signed height, rounded independently to six decimal places. Metric readouts retain their existing metre/kilometre format; other Imperial readouts retain decimal feet unless explicitly switched to this formatter. Non-finite heights and magnitudes beyond the formatter's exact integer-inch range are unavailable. These display choices never alter physical coordinates or express source uncertainty.

The query encloses a WGS84 geodesic disk without approximating the requested distance by a spherical distance. With geodetic latitude `φ`, longitude `λ`, semimajor axis `a` and eccentricity squared `e²`,

```text
ds² = M² dφ² + N² cos²φ dλ²
M = a(1−e²)/(1−e²sin²φ)^(3/2), N = a/sqrt(1−e²sin²φ)
Mmin = a(1−e²)
ds² ≥ Mmin² (dφ² + cos²φ dλ²).
```

Therefore an ellipsoidal disk of radius `R` lies inside a cap of angular radius `δ = min(π, R/Mmin)` on the sphere of *geodetic parameters*. This lower metric bound is an enclosure proof, not a replacement Earth shape. Latitude bounds are `φ0±δ`, clipped to the poles. A cap reaching a pole uses all longitudes. Otherwise its longitude half-span is `asin(sinδ/cosφ0)`. Dateline-crossing bounds split into two ordered rectangles. A `10⁻¹⁰ rad` outward guard protects floating-point bounds; it does not assert submillimetre geographic accuracy. [NGA](https://earth-info.nga.mil/index.php?dir=wgs84&action=wgs84) supplies the defining WGS84 constants.

Every one-degree source tile intersecting these rectangles is requested, plus east/south support tiles needed for shared edge posts. Consequently downloaded and modeled whole-tile coverage can exceed the circle and its bounding rectangles. This first importer does not clip a circular terrain edge. A 128-source-file interactive limit is a resource constraint: requests exceeding it must use a smaller radius. The network estimate sums source Content-Length values; cache reuse reduces new transfer. Sizes use decimal kB/MB/GB and exclude small metadata, notices and in-memory mesh overhead.

## Native registration, sampling and vertical conversion

The strict Rust TIFF decoder requires one Float32 metre band, DEFLATE/floating-point prediction, EPSG:4326 geographic degrees, `RasterPixelIsPoint`, and the expected north-up source dimensions, scale and tie point. It reads original source posts, not resampled overview heights. A malformed, unregistered, nonfinite or missing retained sample rejects the import; it is never converted to zero. Raw GeoTIFF tie points describe point samples directly: no additional half-pixel shift is applied. This differs from GDAL's corner-affine interface, which [already compensates for PixelIsPoint](https://gdal.org/en/stable/development/rfc/rfc33_gtiff_pixelispoint.html).

For a tile with nominal southwest index `(S,W)`, the first retained source post is `(latitude S+1, longitude W)`. Latitude spacing is one arc second. Longitude spacing is 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 5 or 10 arc seconds, according to the provider's latitude bands. The AWS release removes duplicate east and south edge posts; those posts come from the adjoining tiles. Missing support data trims the affected edge instead of extrapolating it. The exact pole row is excluded because its longitudes collapse to one position, which would create degenerate regular-grid triangles.

The importer selects a background angular lattice using integer half-arcsecond units. Its step divides 7,200 units per degree and is a multiple of every participating native longitude spacing and the latitude spacing. Shared retained edges therefore use the same native source posts, including at latitude-band transitions and the dateline. The smallest valid background step keeping at most **300,000 grid vertices** is chosen. On the 100-mile request around the startup position this is 30 arc seconds: substantial nearby relief can be lost by that background alone.

The current importer therefore adds bounded local detail around the **request center**, using a radius up to 5km and no greater than the requested download radius. It refines source-aligned background cells intersecting the conservative geographic enclosure of that radius. Fine spacing is the smallest common original-source lattice that divides the background step; at low latitudes this retains every one-arcsecond source post. At latitude-band transitions, a common step can be coarser than either individual axis so every retained coordinate exists in the source row that owns it. No absent source height is synthesized. This region remains centered where the request was made; it does not silently move while flying.

Fine cells retain the fixed northwest–southeast diagonal. Their neighboring coarse cells share the complete fine edge-post sequence and fan to an additional **original interior source post**, producing a conforming transition with no overlap, skirt or height interpolation masquerading as a source sample. Original background posts remain unchanged. Extra source posts are conservatively budgeted to 200,000, for at most **500,000 canonical vertices** overall. If that budget would be exceeded, the detailed radius is repeatedly reduced; an unresolved oversized allocation is omitted and reported as zero local coverage. The actual local/background spacings, radius, additional-post count and retained EGM2008 height range are reported after import.

One complete TIFF is decoded at a time, then only requested source posts are retained. The GPU and physical queries use the same final refined triangles. Detail outside the reported neighborhood remains coarse, and peaks, narrow valleys, buildings and channels between retained posts can disappear. Even native GLO-30 does not resolve sub-grid terrain. Heights are never exaggerated to make a flat coast look mountainous. Copernicus source ocean posts near the startup coordinate are zero relative to EGM2008; nearby nonzero structures/land relief and the geoid conversion are separate facts. A completed download does not imply that every current eye location has a nonflat ground surface.

Source height `H` is relative to EGM2008. The ECEF conversion requires ellipsoidal height `h`:

```text
h(φ,λ) = H_EGM2008(φ,λ) + N_EGM2008(φ,λ).
```

Here `N_EGM2008` is geoid undulation, unrelated to the curvature radius `N` above. The sign follows [GeographicLib's geoid-height relation](https://geographiclib.sourceforge.io/html/GeoidEval.1.html). The bundled native 2.5-arc-minute grid and its existing bilinear interpolation supply the correction; [geoid.md](geoid.md) documents registration and uncertainty. The TIFF's horizontal GeoKeys do not themselves certify its vertical datum: that comes from the identified source product and retained metadata. EGM2008 is not a live tide surface. Negative ellipsoidal heights are valid and must not be clipped to `h=0`.

## Shared physical surface, fallback and limits

Each retained `(φ,λ,h)` becomes an f64 ECEF vertex. Regular background/fine cells use the northwest–southeast diagonal, with the source-post transition fans described above. Ray ranges, collision, ground placement and terrain skyline calculations use these same Cartesian triangles and BVH. This piecewise planar surface interpolates between retained posts; it is not a separate bilinear height map or an exact continuous terrain reconstruction. The renderer retains the canonical terrain vertices and triangles. Source category lookup remains independent and does not flatten mapped ocean, lake or river heights. A dedicated imported-ground query distinguishes actual canonical coverage beneath the observer from h=0 fallback; categorical water labels alone cannot establish terrain coverage.

The reference ellipsoid is masked where the actual imported mesh occupies the geodetic ground query, including negative terrain. Outside that footprint, the fallback is explicitly `h=0`; it is not measured terrain. Missing tiles or support strips can create a visible elevation discontinuity and can dominate the apparent horizon. No connecting wall, fabricated slope, bathymetry or ocean extension is added. Finite display tessellation can differ at the regional edge; the f64 canonical queries remain authoritative.

The wide-area startup-location check provides a concrete example. The available N29W095 mesh ends at latitude `29.0083333333°` on its retained 30-arcsecond background lattice because southern support from N28W095 is unavailable. Its sampled ocean heights are `H=0`, which become roughly `h=−27 m` after the local EGM2008 correction. Immediately beyond the physical footprint, the model returns to `h=0`. This can leave a thin visible water-surface seam even though geographic water classification continues across it. It is an explicit source-coverage/fallback discontinuity, not evidence of a surveyed wall or a reason to flatten measured source heights. Switching Elevations off puts both sides on the same smooth ellipsoid.

### Independent categorical coverage

An elevation download also requests each planned tile's licensed native Water Body Mask. These UInt8 PixelIsPoint rasters are validated separately from Float32 heights and retain every source category in exact row run-length encoding. Missing masks do not invalidate otherwise valid elevation tiles, and missing elevations do not imply ocean. The same geographic category lookup is used on imported terrain and on smooth WGS84 after Elevations is switched off; the raw masks remain cached. Replacing an area replaces its immutable mask layer without retaining a chain of earlier requests.

The four bundled reconstructed borders have priority, followed by native downloaded WBM, then source-scale global Natural Earth vector context. The latter supplies a recognizable global land/ocean map even where ocean-only Copernicus tiles are unavailable. Its bounded adaptive index preserves original polygon segments instead of the preceding 0.01-degree raster staircase. It does not enlarge actual elevation coverage or supply water heights. This distinction fixes the former small rectangular category island inside a much larger valid DEM request. CPU surface queries and the GPU use the same declared categories, with separately limited display arithmetic. [Water classification](water.md) specifies native-post ownership, vector winding and provenance.

Installing or toggling a scene preserves the observer pose when clear. If the new surface would contain the feet, the application raises them along geodetic up by an explicitly reported amount. Exact GPS navigation still rejects below-surface destinations rather than silently changing entered heights. Terrain skyline/L/V calculations use bounded background work and hide results from obsolete scene snapshots; their finite search can miss narrow silhouette branches. [Visibility](visibility.md) and [guide mathematics](horizon-guides.md) describe those limits.

Eligible atmospheric viewing intersects these same canonical triangles with curved rays. Reference-ellipsoid crossings are masked by actual canonical coverage, including negative terrain; the physical elevations remain unchanged. The fixed forecast-column anchor belongs to the active source/scene snapshot. Numerical or display limits remain explicit and must not mix a curved picture with geometric measurement. See [the optical model and limits](refraction.md).

# GPU presentation mathematics and limits

With atmospheric refraction disabled, the native view uses Bevy 0.20's PBR raster pipeline at the physical framebuffer resolution, with four-sample MSAA. With a validated active weather profile, a separate curved-ray pass traces the canonical terrain, reference ellipsoid and visible survey geometry at native pixel resolution, as described below. The default window is 1920×1080. There is no low-resolution Earth image enlarged to fill the screen. Earth uses source-backed categorical colors; the Moon uses the registered NASA maps described below.

With downloaded elevations disabled, the authoritative physical surface is the analytic f64 WGS84 ellipsoid at `h=0`. Enabling an imported [Copernicus DSM region](topography.md) replaces its covered surface with accepted canonical f64 source triangles, using the documented EGM2008-to-ellipsoid conversion. Outside finite imported coverage the analytic fallback remains. Separate source-derived boundaries select materials and suppress grass; they do not themselves supply elevations. The GPU has finite tessellation, f32 arithmetic and shadow-map resolution. Numerical readout precision must not be interpreted as a guarantee that every displayed chord exactly coincides with the curved scientific surface.

## Coordinate precision

Let the floating origin be `O` in ECEF metres, with local geodetic east `E`, north `N` and up `U`. Before conversion to f32, a geographic vertex `P` is transformed as

```text
X = dot(P − O, E)
Y = dot(P − O, U)
Z = −dot(P − O, N)
```

East/up/south is right handed because `E × U = −N`. Camera directions use the same basis without subtraction. The camera's actual eyes include the selected standing/crouching offset. Near Earth, a position-dependent movement threshold between 80 m and 20 km requests a new floating origin. While its meshes are prepared, the camera continues moving in the previous coherent frame; the threshold is therefore a request threshold, not a hard bound on the current camera-to-origin distance. Nearby f32 coordinate spacing normally remains much smaller than the roughly half-metre spacing of raw Earth-radius f32 positions, but fast movement during preparation can reduce that advantage. Far-side planet vertices and astronomical body positions remain less precise than local terrain; this is a presentation boundary, not a modification of their f64 source values.

At an eye height of 2,000 km the next coherent mesh snapshot changes only its coordinate anchor to a fixed WGS84 latitude/longitude 0/0 surface frame (eye offset 1.72 m). That frame is retained until descent below 1,000 km. The hysteresis prevents repeated anchor replacement near one threshold. It does not move or rotate the actual camera, change the lens, switch renderer, or alter any scientific coordinate. Earth mesh coordinates remain within approximately one planetary diameter instead of storing astronomical-unit offsets in every surface vertex. The separate camera translation and astronomical positions still have finite f32 precision at large distance; this is not metre-accurate GPU geometry throughout the four-AU navigation domain. Geographic material uniforms use the committed coordinate anchor, not the distant camera location.

Earth tessellation, canonical imported-mesh conversion and categorical-buffer packing run on one background worker. The latest camera pose and optical FOV are applied every frame; they do not wait for a new mesh. A finished snapshot for the current immutable physical scene is committed even if the camera has moved again, then the newest required view is prepared. This avoids cancelling every request during continuous flight or zoom. A completed snapshot replaces the reference, every regional/imported mesh, their local origin and corresponding material coordinates together. Camera, celestial, survey and grass state use that committed frame in the same update. The previous finite tessellation can temporarily remain visible at a stronger magnification; measurement rays and canonical f64 geometry are unchanged.

Results for a different physical scene are discarded. Until a current-scene snapshot is available, incompatible Earth and grass geometry is hidden rather than displayed under the new scene's measurements. Grass bounds and its visible mesh are cleared together when the committed origin changes. Only one Earth job is in flight; subsequent view changes coalesce into the request started after it finishes. This is a coherent main-world commit, not a guarantee of indivisible GPU upload: Bevy removes the previous render asset before preparing a modified mesh and skips drawing an unavailable asset. Temporary missing geometry and finite frame-time spikes remain possible during upload. Background preparation is not a guarantee of constant frame rate. The opt-in view-motion recorder retains every real frame interval and records pending preparation rather than excluding slow or incomplete frames.

## Surface tessellation and LOD

Regional boundary meshes retain the source-derived land/water partition at every viewing distance. Every vertex is generated at WGS84 `h=0`, including reconstructed shoreline samples. The renderer applies the analytic ellipsoid normal to both material copies of a boundary vertex. There is no height- or slope-based land color. The material lookup uses the source hierarchy below; a category changes shading and grass eligibility, never a display vertex or physical elevation.

In smooth mode, the application builds the global and regional meshes together. Along each regional coverage perimeter it takes the union of every existing boundary vertex and every adaptive reference-cell endpoint. Both sides use those same geographic posts and the same `h=0` conversion. Regional perimeter triangles with inserted edge posts are split into a fan about their geographic centroid; each child retains the original material. Ordered edge subdivisions and the centroid partition the original convex parameter-space triangle. This removes regional/global T-junction gaps without an elevation offset or overlapping skirt. Interior coastline triangles are unchanged by this display stitching.

Each reference-cell edge retrieves its posts from the sorted shared latitude/longitude registry with two binary searches. Bottom/right edges retain `[lower, upper)` and top/left edges retain `(lower, upper]` in reverse order. These are the same finite predicates and traversal order as the former whole-line scans; no post, triangle, attribute or tessellation criterion is removed. The optimization reduces CPU assembly work rather than lowering geometric detail.

The screen selection heuristic is

```text
focal_pixels = framebuffer_height / (2 tan(vertical_FOV / 2))
projected_error_estimate = geometric_bound × focal_pixels / minimum_range
```

Near Earth the minimum range uses a containing sphere. For a far preparation at actual eye `E0` with geodetic height `h0`, let `delta=h0/4`. A cell with bounding center C and radius r uses `max(h0-delta, |E0-C|-r-delta)`. The normal foot is the nearest point on the convex WGS84 ellipsoid, so h0 is the eye's minimum surface distance. The triangle inequality and distance-to-set Lipschitz bound keep both terms conservative while the actual eye remains within delta of E0. This reference tessellation can therefore be reused across that neighborhood while the requested focal budget does not exceed its prepared value. Regional/imported canonical triangles are retained unchanged. Coordinate anchor and detail-selection eye are deliberately separate. This is a heuristic in pixels, not a strict off-axis perspective error proof. Very wide fields of view magnify edges differently from the image center.

Reference-ellipsoid cells use geographic latitude/longitude subdivisions. With `a`, `b`, and `e²` from WGS84, let

```text
R = a² / b
q = 1.5 e² / (1 − e²)
K = R sqrt(1 + q²)
angular_span = Δlatitude + Δlongitude             # radians
geometric_chord_bound = K × angular_span² / 8
```

`R` bounds the principal curvature radii. `K` bounds the second derivatives of the ellipsoid's geodetic parameterization; the bound accounts for the changing meridional radius. A Taylor/barycentric interpolation bound supplies the stated chord bound. Each leaf is fan-triangulated through all neighboring edge posts, avoiding T-junction cracks inside the reference mesh.

In smooth mode, the four regional source rectangles partition the reference mesh. Their detailed reconstructed material partition ends at the documented mask boundary; downloaded native categories or coarse global context continue beyond it. Physical height does not change at a category boundary. Scientific range, collision, ground and skyline queries use the analytic ellipsoid rather than either mesh's chords. Finite triangle sag, f32 precision and the display budget remain presentation errors; they are not mountains, ocean depths or scientific elevation values.

The GUI LOD factors are 0.5, 1, 2 and 4, applied to the selection focal length. The effective focal length for mesh selection is capped at 16,000 pixels and reference refinement at 17 subdivision levels. Regional classification meshes retain their complete finite partition rather than simplifying across a coastline. Optical field of view itself is not capped by this mesh budget. These finite limits prevent telescope settings from allocating billions of triangles; high magnification can reveal the finite mesh. No unlimited-resolution claim is made. For an enabled imported region, every accepted canonical source vertex and triangle is retained. The importer selects a reported coarse source lattice plus a finer original-source neighborhood and a stitched transition under its finite vertex budget. The finer shared lattice can remain coarser than one native source axis at latitude-band transitions. The renderer retains every accepted canonical face and performs no second thinning that could discard peaks or mismatch shared edges. Regional and imported faces are partitioned into spatial draw chunks; four classification-region slots do not restrict terrain coverage. The outside reference ellipsoid retains adaptive LOD. Range, collision, grounding and skyline use the same f64 DSM geometry. An empty-reference diagnostic at a 2,000 km equatorial eye selected 46,716 cells at a 935.3-pixel focal budget and 755,400 cells at the 16,000-pixel cap, before perimeter synchronization and fan expansion. The latter still produces millions of triangles and substantial preparation, memory and upload cost; asynchronous preparation does not guarantee smooth maximum-magnification updates.

Every ordinary regional/imported draw chunk has at most 16,384 faces. The worker recursively divides the triangle-centroid set at its median along the largest centroid span. It only partitions and remaps index lists: the complete oriented face multiset and every position, normal and color bit are retained. Shared boundary vertices are copied exactly between chunks. Bounds are computed from the already quantized display positions in f64; after the center is rounded to f32, each half-extent is recomputed against that rounded center, has a positive minimum, and is rounded outward by one representable step. Vertex extrema enclose each complete planar triangle. These per-chunk AABBs let Bevy reject unrelated regions in view and shadow passes instead of treating Texas and Japan as one Earth-spanning box. The reference globe remains a separate non-shadow-casting draw.

Chunk meshes, bounds and the common local origin commit together. Obsolete entities are removed before render extraction; new entities use the same material coordinates and source scene. The earlier finite GPU-upload caveat still applies. Frame capture records the prepared chunk count and previous Bevy visibility eligibility alongside all actual frame intervals; these are not hardware draw counters or GPU execution times. No physical LOD reduction or source-height change is used to improve performance.

Elevated land/water appearance is classified per fragment against the retained geographic AEB triangles, rather than from a coarse DEM face's center. This preserves the curved material borders independently of elevation sampling. Lookup uses the stable local geodetic normal inversion below, not a sloping face normal, with the same source-cell candidate index and signed-edge-distance tolerance as the optical pass. Classification does not level water or displace source heights. The finite coverage edge can step to the h=0 fallback; no fabricated blending slope or skirt conceals it. Rectangular display openings retain a finite difference from exact Cartesian-triangle footprints.

## Frame pacing

The ordinary foreground default targets at most 60 application frames per second; selectable targets are 30, 60, 120 or Unlimited. Unfocused windows target at most 15 and minimized/hidden windows at most 5. These are upper-rate targets, not guaranteed throughput. A monotonic-clock gate runs before Bevy's time systems. It waits until the previous actual frame start plus the selected period, rounded upward to an integral nanosecond. Late frames start without an extra delay and reset the anchor to their actual start; the system never schedules catch-up bursts. It does not clamp, scale or replace elapsed time, so simulation and astronomical playback retain their existing real-clock semantics, including Bevy's possible pipelined render timestamp.

The explicit QA flag `--frame-pacing-unrestricted` bypasses both foreground and background caps without changing the existing presentation/VSync policy. Before/after rendering-cost comparisons must use matching effective pacing, focus, present mode and view; a lower GPU utilization caused by a frame cap is reported separately from faster rendering. Minimized/background reductions and operating-system wake-up jitter remain visible in real frame-interval recordings.

## Rectilinear and equidistant optics

### Continuous ground-to-space camera and orbital annotations

The same canonical Observer, full camera triad and active Lens drive the world renderer at every supported flight height. There is no automatic recentering, pose fit, fixed-60-degree lens substitution or separate orbital body pass. Imported ground, ordinary atmospheric eligibility, source-derived categories, physical-size apparent Sun/Moon spheres and the star catalogue retain their existing rendering paths. The old analytic inspection prototype remains only in archived numerical fixtures; it is not a runtime camera mode. The [orbital science model](orbital-overview.md) defines the dated geometric trail and CIP/plane context.

Annotations become eligible when the angular radius of Earth's enclosing equatorial-radius sphere is at most 90% of the current vertical angular half-field. This visibility criterion is a display choice, not a mode change or a claim that Earth is centered in the view. The canonical ECEF eye and all three view axes are rotated into Earth-relative ICRF in f64. Keeping the eye Earth-relative avoids adding and then subtracting an astronomical-unit heliocentric translation. The dated path subtracts the current geometric Earth center; Sun/Moon locator positions instead use the same apparent centers as the world renderer. These are geometric orbit context over the current apparent viewing model, not a second set of rendered bodies. For this inspection camera, receive-time light paths and parallax use the actual eye, while aberration uses Earth's barycentric translation plus the corotating WGS84 h=0 reference observatory at the eye's geographic latitude/longitude (or translation alone when Earth following is disabled). The same convention applies continuously at all heights. It avoids treating a virtual AU-height inspection camera as a rigidly corotating relativistic receiver. Earth terrain remains instantaneous geometric ECEF, so this bounded Earth-reference sky is still an inspection approximation, not a fully retarded or Lorentz-transformed extended Earth image. See [the receiver convention](continuous-flight.md).

The ephemeris path, CIP axis, instantaneous orbital-plane normal and local plane circle are depth-independent annotations. They use the actual rectilinear, telescope or equidistant lens, physical framebuffer aspect, full camera roll and logical UI scaling. Chords are clipped against the camera-front plane, aperture and viewport using the same reviewed projector as the tape. Fisheye chords use its bounded adaptive screen polyline; each annotation collection also has a finite 16,384-segment display budget. The path joins dated samples and is not forcibly closed. If the current epoch lies strictly inside one sample interval, that display segment is split at the exact current Earth position. Remaining joins are finite straight chords, not exact continuous orbital arcs.

The local plane circle starts at 1.5 Earth equatorial radii; its axis and normal strokes extend 1.3 times that circle radius. When needed for legibility, the common annotation radius grows to a nominal 50 physical pixels at Earth's depth, with an explicit enlarged-direction label. This changes annotation lengths only. The CIP direction is distinct from the polar-motion-affected geographic body axis, and its tilt to the instantaneous orbital normal and complementary unsigned angle to the plane are separately labeled. Tiny body locator rings and all text are annotations; they neither enlarge physical bodies nor become range targets.

An optional explicit Fit whole orbit command constructs a Sun-centered enclosing sphere for the current bodies and retained dated path, places the same canonical Observer outside it, and preserves the current lens. Its distance is `R/sin(theta)` where theta is 90% of the limiting vertical/horizontal angular half-field. The fixed inspection direction combines 0.6 radial and 0.8 orbital-normal components. The result is transformed into the existing ECEF observer domain; an unrepresentable narrow-lens fit is rejected instead of silently changing magnification. This deliberate repositioning is separate from ordinary continuous flight. Fit Earth is likewise an explicit navigation command.

Caption layout measures the actual UI font in logical pixels and packs all body, axis, normal, tilt and legend backings together. It reserves the scientific anchors, adds leader strokes where needed, and uses bounded candidate placement with explicit margins. Accepted caption rectangles do not overlap; a caption without a usable candidate is omitted in a crowded or tiny viewport. This display-only layout never moves a body center or direction stroke.

### Shared world optics

The normal and telescope views use Bevy's vertical-field-of-view perspective matrix. Camera near clipping is 0.02 m; far culling is 4×10¹² m. This encompasses the supported Earth–Moon–Sun scene but is finite.

Fisheye retains six square perspective targets at the framebuffer height, with directions `+F`, `+R`, `−F`, `−R`, `+V`, `−V`, where `F`, `R`, `V` are camera forward, right and up. Wide fields use 90° source projections; narrow fields adapt the forward projection as described below. Only targets sampled by the compositor render. Up/down face up vectors are `−F` and `+F`. The shader computes the ideal equidistant direction:

```text
x = aspect × (2u − 1)
y = 1 − 2v
ρ = sqrt(x² + y²)
θ = ρ × vertical_FOV / 2
direction = (x sinθ/ρ, y sinθ/ρ, cosθ)
```

At the center the direction is forward. Pixels with `ρ > 1` are outside the circular aperture. For wide fields, the dominant direction component selects a cube face; division by that component recovers its perspective UV coordinates. The supported full FOV is at most 180°, so the rear face is never selected; default fisheye renders five views. For half-FOV strictly below `π/4 − 0.002` radians, forward dominates every transverse component and only that view renders. The guard retains side views near the tie: its cosine-minus-sine margin exceeds the two allowed `2⁻¹¹` trigonometric errors plus ordinary f32 arithmetic. The error allowance follows the [WGSL accuracy requirements](https://www.w3.org/TR/WGSL/#floating-point-accuracy).

In a single-view field, the forward perspective FOV is `f32(1.00002 × selected_FOV_radians)`. Its source scale `s=cos(source_FOV/2)/sin(source_FOV/2)` follows the pinned Bevy/glam matrix's f32 operation order. Compositor coordinates become `uv = ((direction.x,−direction.y) × s)/direction.z/2 + 1/2`. This preserves the equidistant launch direction while using the source texture for the selected angular field, instead of enlarging a tiny crop of a fixed 90° image. The relative 20 ppm projection pad covers finite aperture-edge rounding without imposing a fixed angular floor on telescope zoom. The narrow shader evaluates `sin(θ)/θ` through its `θ^10` Taylor term and `cos(θ)` through `θ^12`, avoiding the fixed absolute sine error allowance at tiny angles. On `|θ|<π/4`, the next-term bounds are below `9×10^-12` and `4×10^-13`; f32 arithmetic remains the dominant finite approximation. The same helper supplies star-candidate directions; physical one-pixel star ownership still uses the selected equidistant lens.

Depth testing, source geometry, target dimensions and CPU measurement rays are unchanged. The source camera's actual focal length now drives the existing finite terrain-detail criterion and 16,000-pixel cap. Bilinear reprojection is still finite: with equal source/display heights, the central source-to-display sampling ratio is `half_FOV/tan(source_FOV/2)`, about 1 at a 1° field and about 0.79 near the single-view cutoff. The change removes the severe narrow-field undersampling; it does not promise unlimited detail or a strict pixel-error bound at extreme telescope settings.

## Grass and range

The blades are original illustrative geometry, not surveyed vegetation or a land-cover classification. A jittered global latitude-row lattice with longitude spacing derived from each row fixes placement to Earth; longitude indices wrap at the date line. Every root is independently projected onto the active canonical surface: analytic `h=0` or the enabled imported source triangles. Only mapped, unmixed land admits roots: water, mixed boundaries and unclassified locations are rejected before entering the placement cache. Both primary and basal roots receive this check. The source water mask does not establish that eligible land is actually grassland. Primary blades stand 7.5–19 cm tall; a deterministic hash retains approximately a quarter of these taller stems. Three more strongly leaning leaves per eligible cell stand 4.5–9 cm tall, have their own nearby roots and fade at individual distances of 4–6 m. This puts most geometry into low turf near the observer. Curved blades and their material do not change the ellipsoid or flight collision.

Each blade has nine vertices and seven triangles, with a tapered width, curved resting shape, individually varied color and bounded wind. Quality sets maximum visibility distances of 12, 18, 22 or 26 m; individual cutoff distances vary to avoid a common visible ring. Over the last 3 m before a blade's cutoff, cubic smoothstep shrinks its geometry about its anchored root. The fade uses the actual observer's eye position for both the forward and shadow passes. A 5 m generation guard allows camera motion between static mesh uploads.

The CPU uploads a resting position and four displacement coefficient vectors per vertex. Every frame it supplies four bounded sine/cosine weights. The GPU evaluates the same linear combination in both its color and shadow vertex shaders. Base coefficients vanish, so wind does not detach roots. A conservative culling box includes the root and resting position plus/minus the sum of absolute coefficient components, with outward floating-point padding. This contains every wind state and root-relative fade state. Static mesh uploads occur only when generation, origin or quality changes; wind updates do not rebuild the whole mesh on the CPU. Normals follow the curved resting blade and are an approximation during deformation.

Canonical root queries are cached by fixed spatial cell, and the sight query uses cached conservative blade bounds before exact triangle tests. A background task prepares the complete resting mesh, blade list and bounds. A completed snapshot is accepted only for the current immutable terrain scene, generation and an observer within 4 m of its generation point. Scene, origin, quality and enable changes invalidate pending work. Normal movement keeps the previous coherent visible/range snapshot while a replacement is prepared; scene or origin changes clear both together. Fast movement can temporarily reach beyond that field's prepared coverage. Briefly absent decorative grass during preparation does not alter terrain geometry or measurements. Snapshot readiness is exposed to screenshot and frame-pacing checks.

The sight query uses those same quantized local attributes, current f32 wind weights and fade definition. A double-sided ray/triangle intersection returns the closest blade only when it is nearer than the authoritative terrain. GPU fused arithmetic and rasterization may differ by small rounding amounts from the CPU query. The HUD identifies decorative grass separately. This measures generated geometry, not a claim that real grass occupies that location. Thin-leaf transmission, wind and material colors are illustrative, not measured vegetation or weather. A daylight-gated diffuse sky-fill term softens dark back-facing leaves and ground shadows. It is modulated by material color, multiplied by camera exposure once, and added to the lit output before fog and tone mapping. The ground term also has a bounded upward-hemisphere weight. These artistic shading terms do not alter geometry, shadow silhouettes or sight intersections and vanish at night or when the astronomy frame is unavailable.

## Classified water presentation

Within the retained AEB regions, source preprocessing reconstructs supported binary native-cell land/ocean boundaries using outer-edge crossings and bounded G1 curve samples; [water reconstruction](water.md) defines the retained boundary model and its source limits. The height-free boundary asset stores geographic parameters and material classes. In smooth mode, runtime vertices are generated on the WGS84 ellipsoid; the joint mesh preserves the regional partition before its faces are combined under the shared classifier material. The renderer applies no independent coastline smoothing and places no overlapping flat water plane. Any boundary still classified as mixed retains land material and suppresses grass. Native source resolution and the declared reconstruction model still limit shoreline position; added vertices do not constitute additional surveyed detail. In smooth mode, measurement and collision hit the analytic ellipsoid, then classify the hit in the same geographic boundary partition, independently of finite GPU triangles.

The source priority is the retained curved AEB regional partition, then a downloaded native Copernicus WBM post, then the bundled Natural Earth 1:10 million context. The latter now uses the original cartographic polygon segments, not the previous center-sampled 0.01-degree grid and not an extension of the native survey resolution. Its enclosed inland-water category is kept distinct from ocean. Source metadata, licenses and limitations are described in [water classification](water.md). The same hierarchy remains available when elevations are switched off. Hiding mapped land/water bypasses all three appearance sources.

Native WBM categories are stored losslessly as per-row runs: each `u32` is `(exclusive_end_column << 2) | category`, with integer row offsets and a 360-by-180 tile-header lookup. Up to 128 source tiles share a total 16-million-run limit, rather than allocating padded high-resolution texture arrays. Shader lookup binary-searches the row with at most 16 iterations. The archived global raster fixture uses the same run encoding; the current runtime global source uses the bounded vector index below. Buffer addresses, run endpoints and category words remain `u32`. The bounded integer parts of observer coordinate anchors travel in f32 uniforms and are exactly representable at these grid sizes.

Native lookup rounds latitude to the closest one-arcsecond post first, with exact half-post ties north. The owner of a latitude post at an integer degree is the tile immediately south. That owner's latitude-dependent native longitude spacing then determines the nearest longitude post, with ties east and date-line wrapping. The exact owning tile must exist; there is no edge clamp or assumption that a missing tile is ocean. The global fallback instead evaluates its indexed original polygon edges. CPU queries implement these registration rules in f64/integer arithmetic. The shader splits the observer's latitude arcseconds and longitude half-arcseconds into integer anchors and small fractional residuals before adding local angular changes. Global one-degree root coordinates use the same split. This avoids losing a native post through a large absolute f32 coordinate, but does not eliminate finite angle/normal precision near category boundaries. Scalar packing/lookup fixtures and native screenshots are separate checks.

Global vector lookup uses 360×180 one-degree root cells and at most 20 child descents. A constant leaf returns its stored category; a boundary leaf contains at most 32 original directed segment fragments in normalized local coordinates. It follows the reviewed seed-to-query signed-winding calculation in [water classification](water.md). The index cells do not become coastlines. Only query longitude wraps; source segments retain their original geographic interpolation. Integer node/edge addresses and bit-preserved f32 local segment coordinates share one bounded buffer. The current source needs nine subdivision levels. Finite f32 positions can change an exact-edge tie; this is distinct from the removed kilometer-scale raster staircase. Regional AEB curves and native WBM keep their higher priority.

Both geometric PBR and the eligible curved-ray mode use this shared categorical shader. Smooth-mode geometry still comes from the joint regional/reference builder; its existing land/water faces are joined under one classifier material without moving vertices or changing shared edges. Imported terrain retains its accepted physical triangles. Category discontinuities can remain visible where datasets have different resolution or acquisition dates; they are material boundaries, not missing terrain or changes in height. A separate physical height seam can remain at the edge of an imported DSM footprint: for example, a source ocean value `H=0` becomes ellipsoidal `h=N`, whereas a missing ocean-only tile retains the existing `h=0` fallback. Continuous water classification does not erase that datum/coverage difference or invent missing bathymetry.

With elevations disabled, ocean, lake, river and land share `h=0`: an idealized ellipsoid, not mean sea level, geoid, tide, depth or bathymetry. With elevations enabled, water appearance follows the accepted DSM surface without an added flat plane. Outside retained regional curves, downloaded native masks and the separately identified coarse global context supply appearance categories; missing native source tiles are never assumed to be ocean.

The original opaque water material uses Bevy PBR, ordinary terrain shadow maps, four-sample MSAA and the same local eclipse field as land. Its color and chosen dielectric normal-incidence reflectance `F0=0.02` are appearance parameters, not measurements of local water optics. Bevy maps its reflectance control `r` through `F0=0.16 r²`, so the supplied control is `sqrt(0.02/0.16)`. A perceptual roughness of 0.22 keeps highlights finite. There is no photographic texture, bottom visibility or refracted underwater scene.

Animated ripples alter only the shading normal. They do not move water vertices, change the horizon or alter ray ranges. Three original periodic slope fields use integer ECEF direction vectors `(1,2,0)`, `(-2,1,1)` and `(1,-1,3)` and wavelength parameters 64, 16 and 4 m. Each divides the 4096 m coordinate period, preserving the pattern through floating-origin rebases. These are illustrative patterns, not a hydrodynamic dispersion model or measured wind. The CPU passes bounded sine/cosine pairs for angular frequencies 0.33 and 0.61 radians/s; angle addition supplies the third phase without an ever-growing f32 timestamp.

Before periodic wrapping, screen derivatives estimate the ECEF footprint. Each slope fades between phase footprints of 0.2 and 0.8 radians per pixel, suppressing distant shimmer without claiming an exact bandlimit. The combined slope is projected into the surface tangent plane and bounded to length 0.07 before normalizing the perturbed normal. There is no high-frequency color noise or geometric wave displacement.

An explicitly artistic sky-reflection approximation reflects the eye direction about this shading normal and uses the sky sphere's original colors. Its horizon brightness factor is `0.8+0.2 smoothstep(−0.08,0.08,R_up)`, where `R_up` is the reflected unit direction's local-up component. This continuous transition avoids a brightness jump as ripples reflect across the horizon, especially at telescope magnification; it is not measured horizon radiance. Schlick's weight `F=F0+(1−F0)(1−N·V)^5` blends its angular strength; the dot product is clamped to `[0,1]`. It does not reflect shoreline objects, clouds or catalog stars and is not a complete energy-conserving water radiative-transfer model. Direct PBR illumination receives local solar visibility once; diffuse fill uses local artistic eclipse response and camera exposure once. The reflected sky gradient is already in the unlit sky's scene-linear display space, so it is not multiplied by camera exposure again. Its eclipse response is evaluated locally rather than applying the observer's eclipse factor twice.

## Procedural ground material

The ground shader adds original turf, soil and stone shading without changing any terrain vertex. Four periodic value-noise bands use cell widths of 16 m, 2 m, 0.125 m and 0.03125 m. They control restrained color variation, roughness and small surface-normal details; these details are not surveyed surface structure or land-cover data.

Material coordinates are reconstructed from the local east/up/south basis and the f64 ECEF floating origin reduced modulo 4096 m. Every cell width divides that period, and integer hash coordinates wrap by the corresponding number of cells. Shifting the floating origin by a period therefore preserves the material pattern, within local f32 precision. This also avoids a separate map projection or a UV seam at the date line.

Each band uses cubic interpolation and its analytic spatial gradient. Screen derivatives are computed before wrapping the coordinate. The band fades toward its mean as its projected footprint grows from 0.06 to 0.22 cell widths, suppressing fine aliasing without claiming an exact mathematical bandlimit. All decorative material variation also fades smoothly between 40 m and 350 m from the camera, leaving a smooth distant surface without artificial speckled geography. The combined shading gradient is projected into the original surface tangent plane and bounded to length 0.35 before the final normal is normalized. It cannot reverse the normal's hemisphere. It changes lighting only: silhouette, analytic ellipsoid shape, ray hits and flight collision retain the underlying surface.

## Celestial geometry, light and stars

The astronomy library supplies apparent ECEF body centers, physical radii and body-to-ECEF rotation matrices. The renderer uses those centers and radii directly in metres; it does not enlarge the Moon or place the Sun at a nearer arbitrary distance. Body orientation is composed with the local coordinate basis. See [astronomy](astronomy.md) for ephemerides, time scales and accuracy limitations.

The Moon's static mean-radius sphere has 384 longitude and 192 latitude segments. NASA's mapped lunar color and non-exaggerated LOLA-derived shading normals replace invented albedo bands. Geometric and refracted paths share equirectangular registration, emission-time attitude and normalized Lommel-Seeliger display lighting, with a small artistic dark-side floor. Source relief affects shading only: canonical radii, ranges, limbs and occultations remain spherical. The Sun retains its original 192 by 96 angular tessellation. The [lunar appearance and axis model](lunar-rendering.md) specifies source registration, filtering, emission-epoch pole/plane tilt and enlarged direction annotations. Neither material is calibrated eclipse photometry, atmospheric extinction or diffraction.

The 3,000 decorative star points come from an original deterministic generator. Their frame follows the supplied ICRF-to-ECEF rotation, but they are not a scientific star catalog, named constellations, measured magnitudes or finite-distance stellar parallax. Both geometric and refracted views use the same fixed directions, colors and identifiers. They are not regenerated from a frame-dependent random seed. Visibility follows an artistic daylight/altitude function, with an additional total-eclipse rule described below. Unlit celestial colors bypass camera exposure in Bevy's standard material and still pass through tone mapping. These display colors are not calibrated photometry.

Each resolved stellar centre owns **one physical framebuffer pixel**. Its continuous projection is floored to integer pixel coordinates; right and bottom viewport boundaries are excluded. This requested display convention is not stellar angular diameter. Camera movement necessarily steps between pixels; finite projection precision, collisions between sources, clipping and tone mapping remain limitations.

The normal/telescope vertex shader projects the fixed centre and emits a rectangle bounded by that pixel's edges, retaining astronomical depth. MSAA samples lie inside the same pixel, with no Gaussian tail. Fisheye face views omit stars; the final equidistant pass stamps pixels after cubemap filtering. Sky alpha marks unobstructed sky while opaque Earth, grass and finite bodies mask stars. Optical pixels use the finite inverse solve below. Scintillation changes intensity, never position or pixel ownership.

Strength follows an explicitly assumed **Hufnagel–Valley 5/7 reference turbulence column** and weak plane-wave Rytov integral, from [Pirandola, Physical Review Research 3, 023130, Appendix C](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevResearch.3.023130):

```text
Cn²(z) = 0.00594 (21/27)² (10⁻⁵ z)^10 exp(−z/1000)
         + 2.7×10⁻¹⁶ exp(−z/1500) + 1.7×10⁻¹⁴ exp(−z/100)
σ²_zenith(h) = 2.25 (2π/λ)^(7/6) ∫ₕ^∞ Cn²(z) (z−h)^(5/6) dz
λ = 550 nm
σ²(h,e) = σ²_zenith(h) / max(sin(e),sin(35°))^(11/6)
```

Lengths are metres and Cn² has units m^(-2/3). This assumes a point receiver and planar incoming wave, with weak-theory variance below one. Geodetic eye height is transplanted onto the reference column; negative heights use its ground value. Below 35° the slant factor is held fixed: an explicit display continuation, not validated near-horizon turbulence. There is no artificial 40 km cutoff; the remaining turbulence above high observers becomes exponentially negligible. Expanding `(h+x)^10` evaluates the integral using 11 positive gamma moments. Neither mean GFS weather nor a terrain download measures local optical turbulence.

The positive original temporal process is

```text
a = sqrt(2[(1+σ²)^(1/4)−1])
B(t) = ∏ᵢ₌₁⁴ [1+a sin(2π fᵢt+φstar,i)]
f = (0.73,1.17,1.91,2.71) Hz
```

Independent uniform phases give ensemble mean 1 and variance `(1+a²/2)^4−1=σ²`. The frequencies and fixed hashed phases are illustrative, not a measured turbulence spectrum. The reference range keeps a<1, giving positive bounds `(1−a)^4` and `(1+a)^4`. Stars retain original colors and intrinsic brightness, not measured magnitudes. Calendar pause/fast-forward does not accelerate this presentation clock.

The preceding exposure is averaged jointly through 81 Fourier terms: each signed frequency combination `Σsᵢfᵢ`, sᵢ∈{−1,0,1}, receives `sinc(πΔtΣsᵢfᵢ)` at midpoint `t−Δt/2`. Multiplying independently averaged factors would omit cross frequencies. Integer centihertz combinations avoid cancellation ambiguity; midpoint phases are reduced in f64 before upload. This reduces temporal aliasing without guaranteeing alias-free arbitrary frame sampling. Tone mapping and quantization change final pixel statistics; the variance statement applies before display processing. `--star-phase` provides deterministic instantaneous verification phases.

[NASA's qualitative explanation](https://starchild.gsfc.nasa.gov/docs/StarChild/questions/question26.html) describes atmospheric twinkling and stronger low-elevation fluctuations. [Osborn et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.06921) explains the need for measured turbulence and instrument/exposure parameters for quantitative prediction. This conditional reference model does not provide those measurements, stochastic wave propagation, seeing-induced position wander, finite-pupil effects or chromatic scintillation.

Earth sunlight uses one global directional approximation at every flight height: the geometric geocentric Sun direction determines illumination, while the apparent direction from the actual eye determines where the Sun is viewed. This prevents distant observer movement from reversing Earth's light direction. It omits finite solar parallax across the surface and the retarded-versus-geometric direction difference; lunar phase retains its separately supplied Moon-to-Sun illumination. It is illustrative lighting, not exact photometry. Bevy uses four cascaded 4096² shadow maps, with cascades extending to 12 km. The first cascade ends at 12 m for nearby grass. These local shadow maps are disabled at actual eye heights of 1,000 km or more and restored below that height. Their finite reach is already short compared with that altitude; additionally, AU-scale f32 camera translations can collapse a 12 m cascade depth interval to zero in Bevy's corner-extrema calculation. This is a display policy, not removal of geometry or of the separate pointwise eclipse field. Terrain and moving grass cast these shadows; the global reference ellipsoid does not, because an Earth-sized caster destroys the depth precision needed by nearby blades. Its Lambert orientation and local nighttime light policy supply its day/night appearance. Bevy's default depth/normal biases mitigate shadow acne. Terrain bounds update with its geometry; grass uses the explicit wind-bounded culling box described above.

Shadows beyond the configured range, finite-map bias/filter errors and atmospheric scattering are not exact physical simulations. An original smooth sky-color gradient is drawn on a sphere at 3.5×10¹² m, behind the modeled bodies and decorative stars; it is a background, not a physical atmosphere or range target. Its color, warmer low-Sun tint, hemisphere fill and ambient-light level are illustrative clear-sky display choices. Bevy's exponential distance fog gives the terrain restrained aerial perspective; it is not local weather data. Sun, Moon, stars and the sky material explicitly disable this fog. No photographic LUT asset is needed by the selected `SomewhatBoringDisplayTransform` tone mapper.

Optional weather-informed atmospheric refraction uses a separate viewing-ray path. The PBR sky and fog described above remain the straight-ray mode's artistic presentation; neither is itself a refractive model. See [the optical model and weather inputs](refraction.md).

## Survey prisms and tape annotations

Survey markers use the exact oriented-box corners supplied by the [survey geometry model](survey.md). Each prism is 10 by 10 international feet in horizontal cross-section and 100 feet high, with a straight local geodetic-up axis. Its base centre is anchored once to the active canonical ground surface. The renderer subtracts its floating origin in f64 before uploading f32 coordinates. Six faces have independent outward normals and twelve triangles; each prism has its own bounds for view and shadow culling. No distance-dependent enlargement, billboard replacement or geometric LOD changes its dimensions. Subpixel geometry and shadow-map resolution remain finite display limits. A ground-surface change clears the existing placement rather than silently retaining outdated foundations.

Paint alternates red and green in one-foot intervals measured along the physical up axis: the bottom interval is red and the 99–100 foot interval is green. Vertex UV coordinates carry this canonical height directly, so the paint does not slide during a floating-origin change. Bottom and top caps use the adjacent band's color. Directional sunlight, existing shadow maps, spatial eclipse attenuation, artistic diffuse sky fill and fog follow the same conventions as the ground material. Paint colors are original illustrative material values, not calibrated reflectance measurements.

Separate number plates label the row `0001`, `0002`, and onward, without extending the measured prism. Original black/white digit geometry has fixed world dimensions and stays depth-tested in the 3D scene. Each plate turns toward the actual eye only around its marker's fixed geodetic-up axis; it never follows camera pitch or roll. Movement updates entity transforms rather than rebuilding the static plate mesh. Its unlit material and absence of cast shadows are annotation choices. The [number-plate derivation](survey.md#number-plates) specifies dimensions, overhead fallback and the separation from physical picking.

Unresolved bands blend instead of retaining unstable one-pixel stripes. For height `t` in feet, the primitive of the red-band indicator is `F(t)=floor(t/2)+min(t−2floor(t/2),1)`. Its box-filtered red fraction is `[F(t+w/2)−F(t−w/2)]/w`, with `w=max(|∂t/∂x|+|∂t/∂y|,10⁻⁴)` from screen-space shader derivatives. Period reduction before subtraction improves finite arithmetic; a footprint contained in one band returns that band's exact color. At widths of at least 1024 feet the filter uses the equal-color mean, whose difference from the specified box average is at most `1/w`. This is a one-dimensional filter over a locally estimated height footprint, not exact two-dimensional pixel integration or a guarantee against all aliasing. It changes paint coverage only, never the marker height or picking geometry.

In geometric mode, the tape is an always-visible screen annotation of the actual picked A–B ECEF chord, with endpoint labels and the unchanged numerical measurement. It has one physical pixel of geometric thickness perpendicular to each projected segment; diagonal raster coverage can occupy more than one row or column. It is clipped to the camera-front plane, optical aperture and viewport. In fisheye, the projected straight spatial chord is generally curved, so a bounded adaptive polyline approximates its screen path. The overlay deliberately ignores Earth/marker depth occlusion and casts no shadows. Its drawing thickness and clipping do not change either endpoint or the distance, and the annotation cannot be picked as a physical object. [Survey geometry](survey.md) defines the finite projection criterion and measurement semantics.

In optical mode, the same physical prism faces and upright number plates are intersected along curved viewing paths. Plates remain decorative geometry: they do not extend the measured prism or become pick/tape targets. Their original procedural digits and world dimensions are retained. Marker paint uses the periodic band integral with a local optical footprint estimate, an appearance heuristic rather than a curved-image Jacobian bound. The ordinary raster marker/plate pass is hidden, preventing a second straight image.

Optical tape projection uses the f64 inverse solver on one branch seeded by the straight endpoint direction, then checks canonical terrain and marker occlusion. It samples the unchanged A-to-B spatial chord in 16 initial intervals, refining visibility changes and midpoint screen curvature beyond 0.2 physical pixel, up to 1025 samples. Narrow visibility windows and other mirage branches can be missed. A 5 mm endpoint contact band accommodates the inverse solver's 2 mm target residual; neither is a total ray-error guarantee. Unresolved shooting is reported, never replaced with a straight projection. Visible segments retain one physical pixel of geometric thickness after logical/DPI conversion. Measured chords and geodesics are unchanged. This optical annotation is depth-aware, unlike the deliberately always-visible geometric tape.

## Weather-informed curved-ray presentation

When a validated profile is active, a fullscreen shader launches the same rectilinear or equidistant lens directions used by the CPU. Telescope magnification changes the ray launch angles directly; this mode does not warp an already rendered straight-ray image or sample the six fisheye images. It integrates the geometric-optics equation through the profile's piecewise-linear phase refractivity `n−1`, with gradient along the local geodetic normal. Physical surfaces are the analytic WGS84 fallback, active canonical terrain triangles and survey marker prisms. No surface is displaced to imitate bending. Decorative grass is hidden in this mode because its finite raster geometry has no matching curved-ray intersector. Switching refraction off restores the PBR/grass path.

The shader keeps positions relative to the actual eyes in east/up/south coordinates. To evaluate geodetic height without subtracting rounded Earth radii, define `M = a Bᵀ Q B`, `g = a Bᵀ Q O`, and `f₀ = a(OᵀQO−1)`, where `Q=diag(1/a²,1/a²,1/b²)` and B has the local basis columns. The CPU forms `f₀` from the known observer height and the exact h=0 footpoint in f64. For local displacement p:

```text
f = f₀ + p · (2g + Mp)
q = g + Mp
h = f / [u·q + sqrt((u·q)² − f(u·Mu))]
u = normalize(q − hMu)
```

Up to five height/normal iterations evaluate the actual geodetic parallel surface locally. After computing the new height and normal, the loop stops early only when all three normal components have exactly the previous bit patterns, including signed zero. The deterministic next iteration would then reproduce the same height and normal; no epsilon or looser convergence threshold is introduced. This is not an ellipsoid with axes `(a+h,b+h)`. Profile knots store `n−1` separately, avoiding loss of small refractivity in an f32 number close to one. The CPU's independent f64 inverse and solver remain authoritative for measurements.

The GPU uses midpoint step doubling, with at most 2 km per proposed step, 4,096 attempted steps and 2,000 km of atmospheric path. Its display error control is `max(0.002 m, 4×10⁻⁷ |p|)` for the position/direction step-doubling estimate; this is a local numerical control, not a bound on accumulated path or skyline error. Ground and atmosphere-top crossings are refined by reintegration. Rays leaving the profile continue from their exit position and tangent. Above-profile rays first bracket and refine the true geodetic-height entry surface. A failed integration or exhausted budget within the active optical mode produces a diagnostic purple pixel, not a fabricated surface hit or sky classification. Native f32 arithmetic, cancellation at long distances and grazing tangency limit display agreement with the separate f64 measurement solver.

Step doubling alone can miss a thin gradient layer. Both solvers therefore also bound the next step by the nearest upper and lower profile knots, using the positive root of `gap = v s + K s²/2`, with signed height velocity v toward that knot and conservative height-curvature bound `K = 1/[a(1−e²) − 10,000 m] + max|dn/dh|`. The display excludes a small knot band `max(10⁻⁵ m, 4×10⁻⁷ |p|)` to avoid repeatedly approaching the same numerically indistinguishable boundary; adjacent layers narrower than four times that band are unresolved. This is an explicit f32 display limit, not permission to flatten a thin weather layer. The f64 solver has its own smaller numerical band and independent convergence criteria.

The application activates this GPU path for geodetic eye heights from -10,000 to 20,000 m, provided the observer is above its canonical surface and the finite scene packet can be prepared. Imported terrain and survey geometry share this path. Outside that operational range it explicitly selects the geometric renderer, geometric range and geometric L/V guides together, while retaining the downloaded weather profile and showing a fallback notice even in clean view. The limit is a conservative presentation policy, not a physical boundary of refraction or a guarantee that every ray inside it resolves. An orbital eye-relative origin made the near-ground profile knots numerically indistinguishable in native testing; supporting those views needs atmospheric-entry rebasing rather than a larger advertised limit.

Graphics-backend eligibility is also shared by rendering and optical measurement clients. The application disables the curved display on DirectX 12 after reproducible full-resolution native device-loss failures; it retains the weather data and explicitly selects geometric viewing and measurements. Restarting with `WGPU_BACKEND=vulkan` requests the backend that completed the full 1920×1080 terrain/marker capture. That finite native success does not guarantee every adapter, ray or frame rate. Successful DX12 compute diagnostics validate their selected trace cases, not the unsupported full graphics pipeline. The retained failure and success records are described in [verification.md](verification.md).

Independent finite coefficient fixtures at four observer latitudes and nearby surface/atmosphere targets found maximum height differences of about 0.071 m for eye heights through 100 km, and about 2.94 m at 10,000 km. These higher-altitude coefficient diagnostics do not extend the operational optical domain. The values include local-position quantization and are fixture results, not universal or accumulated optical-ray error bounds. Near a tangent, even a small height error can move a surface endpoint appreciably.

An independent scalar f32 transcription also traced 18 rays through the retained NOAA profile at 10 km and 20 km eye heights: three headings, directions 0.01° above and below the CPU skyline, and straight down. All resolved and agreed with the f64 ground/sky classification; maximum ground-endpoint and geometric-path differences were about 19.66 m and 35.41 m. These finite grazing cases demonstrate why a high-altitude rendered endpoint is not promised to match the f64 metrology to a metre. They exclude native GPU contraction/transcendental differences and are not global error bounds.

Canonical terrain export retains every original face and BVH order. Each patch has an h=0 local frame; source ECEF positions are subtracted and rotated in f64 before f32 upload. Outward-rounded bounds and a separate patch hierarchy support bounded searches without scanning the full globe. Limits are 128 patches, 512 visible markers and a 120 MiB geometry-buffer budget; a conservative preallocation check can reject a scene before that final payload size. Unsupported scenes report unavailable. The immutable packet is prepared off the frame thread and accepted only for the current scene identity. While pending, the old scene is not shown as the new one and presentation readiness remains false. Eye transforms and marker/plate records use a separate small buffer.

Each interval of length L expands candidate bounds by `max|dn/dh| L²/8`, the actual local midpoint discrepancy and finite transform allowances. Stackless traversal is bounded to 8192 terrain nodes and 2048 marker nodes per attempted interval. Triangle tests translate to one vertex, and midpoint displacement is accumulated locally instead of subtracting two large ray positions. Source-coordinate quantization remains a separate allowance. Candidate rejection uses the supporting face plane and three inward triangle-edge halfspaces, expanded by the tube/band. Both chord endpoints must lie strictly outside one expanded halfspace to reject it; equality stays a candidate. These necessary conditions avoid ill-conditioned near-parallel closest-segment divisions and can retain extra candidates.

The f32 narrow solver has a finite transverse contact band: at least 2 mm, enlarged by eight machine epsilons times the involved transform, edge and interval scales. Thus 2 mm is not a global accuracy claim. A lower transverse derivative `|N dot u_start| - max|dn/dh| L` must exceed 1e-5. Along-ray contact uncertainty is `(band + local curve tube)/lower_derivative`, potentially much larger near grazing incidence. Confirmed plane roots are refined by reintegration and checked against finite triangle edges; bounded transverse endpoint/edge residuals may be accepted within this band. Overlapping uncertainty intervals from distinct patches or physical/material owners remain ambiguous. Same-patch faces share the finite nearest-contact convention. Ambiguous intervals are halved chronologically; unresolved terminal contacts produce purple pixels. Tangencies and arbitrarily thin geometry are not universally resolved. CPU f64 metrology retains canonical geometry and its separate solver policy.

Reference h=0 events are masked only when a normal-line query intersects an actual imported face, including terrain below h=0. Patch rectangles or water categories do not establish physical coverage. Classification uses the analytic geodetic normal at the hit while lighting uses its terrain/prism normal, so slopes do not rotate the geographic map.

At a ground hit, the shader derives geographic angles from the analytic normal and uses the same regional/native/global category hierarchy as geometric rendering. Coarse source cells index the retained regional parameter-space triangles; native row runs and global polygon segments are read through shared bounded shader code. Regional edge tests and geographic reconstruction have finite f32 precision. No category changes the solved optical hit. Curved viewing rays can expose different surface points from straight rays, including turning-ray behavior where supported by the supplied profile and finite integrator.

Sun and Moon intersections use the atmosphere's **exit position and direction** against the existing apparent finite spheres. Using only the exit direction from the original eye would lose the path's finite-body parallax. The supplied ephemeris/apparent-center model is retained: atmospheric optical delay is not fed back into a new coupled retarded-time ephemeris solution. Lunar color and slope normals use the shared NASA-derived maps and explicitly approximate Lommel-Seeliger presentation described above; physical lunar intersections remain spherical. The same original star catalogue and scintillation model are used. A static six-face 32×32 storage index supplies nearby candidates. A finite angular candidate heuristic limits repeated tracing. Each candidate is solved from its fixed stellar direction, observer and profile, independently of the fragment asking for it. Sixteen fixed-point inverse attempts seek an eye launch direction whose vacuum exit tangent matches that stellar direction. Candidate inversion uses only the atmospheric exit map, with the same field, local midpoint integration, knot bounds, top refinement and finite budgets. It does not repeat terrain/prism intersection work for each tentative launch. This exit map is not a visibility test: the final pixel still follows the complete scene trace, and surface pixels and finite-body hits suppress stars before catalogue evaluation. The reference `h=0` is not an obstruction in the candidate map because imported terrain can lie below it; candidates leaving the supported lower atmospheric domain fail. A resolved launch direction projects to one integer pixel.

The inverse accepts an exit-direction chord residual of 3×10⁻⁶, approximately radians nearby. That residual alone corresponds to about 0.93 pixels at 0.2° vertical FOV and 1080 pixels high, and more at stronger telescope magnification. Integration, f32 rounding and inverse conditioning add errors: exactly one displayed pixel does not guarantee one-pixel astrometric accuracy. Failed inversions omit the star. The finite search does not enumerate multiple ducted images or certify arbitrary refractive magnification; it never substitutes a cosmetic warp. Unresolved surface traces retain purple diagnostics. Scintillation uses the solved incoming elevation and does not change ray paths, finite bodies, horizon or ranges.

Radiance in this mode is an explicitly simplified original shader: diffuse land, a restrained periodic detail band, opaque water with a finite ripple normal and sky-reflection approximation, an illustrative sky gradient, and finite haze. Earth day/night and the shared pointwise eclipse model still attenuate direct illumination. Decorative grass shadow maps are absent with the hidden grass. Viewing-ray refraction does not claim full refracted solar illumination, atmospheric scattering/extinction, cloud transport, physically measured water optics, or calibrated brightness. This pass uses one optical trace per native pixel; raster MSAA does not antialias its internal ray-defined coastline. Profile quality, finite pixel sampling, f32 integration and source boundary resolution remain independent limitations.

Shading detail fades using the footprint estimate `path / [focal × max(|N·u|,10⁻⁶)]`, where N is the surface hit normal and u the arriving unit tangent. The grazing factor follows the largest local planar projection stretch and prevents dense ripple stripes at a telescope horizon. A curved optical map can have additional focusing or caustics, so this remains an appearance-filtering heuristic rather than a certified Jacobian bound. It changes neither the ray, hit, horizon, source classification nor measured distance.

## Moving solar-eclipse shadow

The terrain patches, classified water, reference ellipsoid and grass evaluate solar visibility at each rendered fragment. This is a spatial field: the Moon's umbra, antumbra and penumbra move over Earth as the ephemeris time changes. It does not rely on a single observer-level directional-light dimmer or on including the Moon in the nearby terrain shadow maps.

The astronomy library supplies independently retarded Sun and Moon states. Near-Earth surface fields retain the current observer anchor; beyond a geocentric eye distance of one billion metres the surface field is independently anchored at Earth center with corotating surface velocities, preserving its finite spatial-offset domain. The actual observer's separate eclipse query remains anchored at that eye and its selected motion convention. The surface field corrects receive-time light paths using emission velocities. Switching its distant anchor can change the finite linear-state approximation; it is not an exact globally identical field at that threshold. This is a linear-state spatial approximation; a fresh ephemeris sample remains authoritative at its own observer. See [astronomy](astronomy.md) for the timing model, sphere radii, reference sources and limitations.

Before f32 quantization, the field is rotated in f64 into a Sun-aligned frame. Three affine rows transform a rendered local position into the anchor-relative shadow coordinates. This preserves the small lunar transverse offsets relevant to eclipse alignment, instead of subtracting large almost-parallel f32 vectors in arbitrary Earth axes. Sun/Moon vectors, physical radii, velocities and observer velocity are packed in the same frame. The GPU uses the same six light-time iterations as the CPU field, evaluating a difference of distances through a difference-of-squares identity to reduce cancellation.

For a surface point, the sphere angular radii are `α = asin(Rsun / dsun)` and `β = asin(Rmoon / dmoon)`. Directional separation is `δ = atan2(|usun × umoon|, usun · umoon)`, avoiding the loss of precision in `acos` near alignment. A Moon farther away than the Sun does not obscure it. The circular angular-plane overlap uses normalized separation `d = δ/α` and lunar radius `r = β/α`. Disjoint and contained cases are handled explicitly. Partial overlap uses the chord coordinate

```text
a = 0.5 × (d + (1 − r)(1 + r)/d)
h = sqrt(max(0, (1+r−d)(d−(1−r))(d+(1−r))(d+1+r))) / (2d)
A = atan2(h,a) + r² atan2(h,d−a) − d h
V = clamp(1 − A/π, 0, 1)
```

This is the unobscured fraction of a uniform-radiance angular disk. It is a small-angle circular-disk approximation, not spherical-cap solid-angle photometry. It omits solar limb darkening, lunar limb topography, diffraction, corona and atmospheric radiative transfer. Annular eclipses retain a nonzero direct fraction; total eclipses reduce direct sunlight to zero.

The current ground, grass and water materials are non-emissive and receive only sunlight plus solar-derived ambient illumination. Their exposed PBR output, including the existing ordinary shadow-map result, is multiplied by the local `V`. Thus the approximation also scales the built-in solar ambient and transmitted/specular sunlight; independently powered lights would require separating those contributions. The separate artistic diffuse fill uses `0.04 + 0.96 sqrt(V)`, retaining a bounded residual during totality. Material color and exposure are applied once before fog and tone mapping. Water's additional artistic sky reflection follows the same local response, as defined above.

The observer's own visibility fraction affects only the illustrative sky gradient and haze color. It is not applied a second time to the surface or global Sun light. Decorative stars preserve their ordinary night/altitude visibility; the eclipse adds visibility only when the CPU geometric classification is `Total`, using `1 − daylight × atmosphere × (1 − eclipse_stars)`, with `eclipse_stars = 0.96` during totality and zero otherwise. Partial and annular daylight do not expose the star field. This categorical rule changes at contact and is not a stellar-magnitude or eye-adaptation model.

The residual and sky response keep totality readable; they are not measured eclipse brightness, atmosphere or eye adaptation. Floating-point precision, finite render LOD, first-order spatial ephemeris extrapolation and Earth-orientation uncertainty also limit the displayed shadow. Neither screenshots nor the geometric HUD certify exact contact times or real-world photometry.

Independent scalar f32 execution of the shader expressions checks the actual `GpuSolarEclipse` packing against f64 geometry. The contact sweep's maximum absolute visible-fraction error was `1.952×10⁻⁷`. Spatial fixtures for two eclipse epochs, ground and 8,000 km anchors, and 15 km floating-origin rebases differed by at most `2.268×10⁻⁷`; an extreme 1×10⁹ m anchor reached `1.946×10⁻⁵`. These are measured fixture errors, not universal accuracy guarantees. The emulation excludes GPU fused arithmetic, vendor transcendental implementations and rasterization; native captures separately verify shader compilation and visible moving shadows.
